# Code of Conduct

*Sample policy written for the DISHA HR demo. Replace with your company's approved policy before real use.*
Owner: HR · Version 1.0 · Effective 1 January 2026

## 1. What we expect

Everyone at the company is expected to act honestly, treat colleagues and clients with respect, keep company
and client information confidential, and follow the law and company policy.

## 2. Respect at work

Harassment, bullying and discrimination are not tolerated. This covers discrimination on the basis of gender,
caste, religion, region, disability, age, sexual orientation or marital status, and includes behaviour in
person, in writing and in company chat groups.

The company follows the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act,
2013. The Internal Committee can be reached at ic@company.com and complaints are handled confidentially.

## 3. Confidentiality

- Employee data, salary information, client data and unreleased product information are confidential.
- Share information only with people who need it for their work.
- Do not copy company or client data to personal devices, drives or accounts.
- Confidentiality obligations continue after leaving the company.

## 4. Conflicts of interest

Declare to HR any outside employment, consulting, directorship or business interest that could compete with
the company or affect your judgement. Close relatives working for a competitor, client or vendor should also
be declared. Declared conflicts are usually manageable; undeclared ones are a disciplinary matter.

## 5. Gifts and anti-bribery

Do not offer or accept bribes or kickbacks. Gifts or hospitality worth more than ₹2,500 must be declined or
declared to HR. Never offer anything of value to a government official.

## 6. Company property

Use company laptops, accounts and software for work. Reasonable personal use is fine, but the company may
audit its systems. Return all equipment and access on your last working day.

## 7. Honest records

Attendance, leave, expenses and timesheets must be accurate. Marking attendance for someone else, or claiming
expenses that were not incurred, is treated as serious misconduct.

## 8. Speaking up

Report suspected breaches to your manager, to HR at hr@company.com, or anonymously at ethics@company.com.
Nobody who raises a concern in good faith will face retaliation.

## 9. Disciplinary process

Depending on severity, breaches lead to a documented conversation, a written warning, a final warning, or
dismissal. Serious misconduct, including fraud, harassment, data theft or violence, can lead to immediate
dismissal.
